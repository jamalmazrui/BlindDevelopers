# Blind Developers

This directory lists 39 developers who are blind or have low vision, along with software each has built. It is meant as a resource for aspiring blind developers and as a way to find and reach the developers listed.

This is one of three companion directories, alongside [Blind Authors](https://jamalmazrui.github.io/BlindAuthors/) and [Blind Presenters](https://jamalmazrui.github.io/BlindPresenters/), and all three are merged in [Blind Creators](https://jamalmazrui.github.io/BlindCreators/). A related directory, [Blind Apps](https://jamalmazrui.github.io/BlindApps/), turns this one around: it lists every qualifying app by these developers, each with its platform, its technology, and any AI involved, plus statistics on the whole collection.

Each listed app belongs to its developer and keeps its own license. This directory's own text is available under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

A developer is included here when every one of the following is true:

- The developer is blind or has low vision.
- They created or led the development of the app or tool. Software built by a team counts when the developer was a creator or a development lead; everyone listed has at least one app of their own.
- The app or tool is reachable today through a working link to where it can be obtained and used.
- It has been actively developed or maintained in the year 2020 or later. Long-running tools such as JAWS, NVDA, and Emacspeak therefore appear alongside newer projects.

Up to three projects are shown per developer, while Blind Apps shows them all, and each developer's entry ends with a "Profiles:" line — GitHub, LinkedIn, a website, or other relevant pages, listed alphabetically by label.

Developers are listed alphabetically by last name, with the projects grouped by platform in an appendix.

This directory is English-language in scope: it relies on documentation, code, and sources available in English. Blind developers who build and publish in other languages are not represented here.

## Developers (Alphabetical by Last Name)

### Tony Acosta {#dev-acosta}

A creator in the Canary Islands, Spain, who teaches blind iPhone users through hundreds of Canal TifloAcosta videos and podcast episodes.
Profiles: [GitHub](https://github.com/TifloAcosta), [Podcast](https://podcasts.apple.com/es/podcast/canal-tifloacosta/id1567846456), [YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/@tifloacosta).

#### [TifloAcosta App](https://tifloacosta.com/) {#tifloacosta-app}
A free web app in Spanish and English, installable on iPhone, Android, and Windows with no registration, that gathers a growing library of guides, courses, news, and his video catalog, with search and navigation designed for screen reader users. Its source is on [GitHub](https://github.com/TifloAcosta/tifloacosta-app).

### Nicholas Adams {#dev-adams}

A severely sight impaired developer who created a tool to type Universal English Braille on a standard keyboard, with no specialised hardware required.
Profiles: [Documentation](https://nmfa.github.io/BrailleKeyboard-Documentation/).

#### [BrailleKeyboard](https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9mvrrhnbb5xc) {#braillekeyboard}
A Windows app that turns a standard keyboard into a UEB braille keyboard: the F, D, and S keys and the J, K, and L keys map to the six braille dots, and each chord generates ordinary text anywhere in the operating system. It supports contractions and custom wordsign dictionaries.

### Taylor Arndt {#dev-arndt}

Developer and Chief Operating Officer at Techopolis who builds Swift and web apps with accessibility designed in from the start, and co-leads the Accessibility Agents project for AI coding tools with Jeff Bishop.
Profiles: [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/taylor-arndt-3ab16a142/), [Website](https://taylorarndt.com/).

#### [Start Testing](https://starttesting.net/) {#start-testing}
A fully accessible project management platform for organizations, covering projects, tasks, bugs, features, milestones, reviews, releases, and client collaboration, that keyboard and screen reader users can work in throughout; she built it at Techopolis.

#### [Swift Agents](https://github.com/Techopolis/swift-agents) {#swift-agent-team}
Sixteen specialized Swift agents for Claude Code and GitHub Copilot in Visual Studio Code, published by Techopolis, that enforce Swift 6.2 concurrency, SwiftUI, Foundation Models, VoiceOver accessibility, security, and App Store compliance; free and MIT-licensed, with a one-command install. Formerly named swift-agent-team.

#### [wx-accessible-webview](https://github.com/Community-Access/wx-accessible-webview) {#wx-accessible-webview}
A wxPython library, installable with `pip install wx-accessible-webview`, that makes embedded web views report correctly to screen readers such as JAWS and NVDA, solving a long-standing accessibility gap for blind developers building Python desktop apps. Also published on PyPI.

### Jeff Bishop {#dev-bishop}

President of Blind Information Technology Solutions (BITS) and an IT accessibility consultant at the University of Arizona, who co-leads the Accessibility Agents project for AI coding tools with Taylor Arndt.
Profiles: [BITS](https://bits-acb.org/), [Community Access](https://community-access.org/).

#### [GLOW Accessibility Toolkit](https://bits-acb.org/) {#glow}
A BITS accessibility toolkit that audits, fixes, and templates Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Markdown, PDF, and ePub documents to meet accessibility guidelines.

#### [Quill](https://github.com/Community-Access/quill) {#quill}
A free, screen-reader-first writing and document environment for Windows and macOS, co-created with Taylor Arndt, that reads and writes nearly every document format, from Word, EPUB and PDF to braille files and DAISY talking books, with a spoken-echo history, Reveal Codes, and optional AI through a local model; a community module is adding equations that a screen reader user can explore term by term.

#### [Quill Radio](https://quillforall.org/radio.html) {#quill-radio}
A free, standalone, screen-reader-first internet radio app for Windows and the successor to the ACB Radio Tuner: it plays every ACB Media stream out of the box plus tens of thousands of stations worldwide, with nested favorites, recording that survives a dropped connection, a wake-up timer, and NOAA weather radio.

### John Boyer {#dev-boyer}

A software engineer from Minnesota who founded the nonprofit Computers to Help People and led the development of widely used open-source braille software until his death in 2023.
Profiles: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Boyer_(software_engineer)).

#### [Liblouis](https://github.com/liblouis/liblouis) {#liblouis}
The open-source braille translator and back-translator, named for Louis Braille, that produces the braille output used by NVDA, Orca, and many commercial tools.

#### [BrailleBlaster](https://www.brailleblaster.org/) {#brailleblaster}
A free, cross-platform application for transcribing print documents into well-formatted braille.

### Tristen Breitenfeldt {#dev-breitenfeldt}

#### [ART (Accessibility Reporting Tool)](https://github.com/ripplelearning/ART) {#art}
An open-source, local-first workspace for documenting accessibility findings, managing standards such as WCAG 2.1 and 2.2, and exporting reports to Word, Excel, PDF, and HTML, built for full keyboard and screen reader use; she conceived and built it with AI-assisted development.
